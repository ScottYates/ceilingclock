Assembly

Ten steps, from printed parts to a focused image on the ceiling. Allow an evening for the electronics and an hour for final assembly. Highlighted parts in each picture are the ones that step adds; "Open in 3D" shows the same view so you can rotate it.

Test the optics on the bench first. Before printing, hold the LED, a piece of baking paper, the LCD and the lens in a stack in a dark room and aim it at the ceiling. That's the quickest way to confirm the lens and LED brightness suit your room.

Tools: soldering iron, rotary tool or hobby knife (for the LCD window), hot glue, wire strippers, black electrical tape, small pliers (only for removing pins).
Hardware: none. The case goes together with printed quarter-turn lock pins and clips, no screws.

The lock pins work like a camera lens: push the pin in with its wing head along the slot beside the hole, then turn the head a quarter turn either way until it lies crosswise and clicks. Behind the wall a lug on the pin rides up a small ramp, which pulls the head tight, drops into a notch, and a stop keeps it from turning further. The head shows at a glance which pins are locked. To remove one, turn it back along the slot (it takes a firm twist) and pull it out. They're the same as the pins on the VoiceLabels case. If a pin is too stiff to turn, lower RAMP_H (0.4 mm) in enclosure.scad; if it doesn't click firmly, raise it a little, or print the pins in PETG.
