Assembly

Nine steps, from printed parts to a focused image on the ceiling. Allow an evening for the electronics and an hour for final assembly. Highlighted parts in each picture are the ones that step adds; "Open in 3D" shows the same view so you can rotate it.

Test on the bench first. The breadboard test page walks through wiring and checking every module before you solder anything. Test the optics too. Before printing, lay the projector display on the table, digits up, and hold the lens about 106 mm above it in a dark room (the bonus step on the breadboard page). That's the quickest way to see whether the projected digits are bright enough for your room.

Tools: soldering iron, hot glue, wire strippers, black electrical tape, small pliers (only for removing pins).
Hardware: none. The case goes together with printed quarter-turn lock pins and clips, no screws.

The lock pins work like a camera lens: push the pin in with its wing head along the slot beside the hole, then turn the head a quarter turn either way until it lies crosswise and clicks. Behind the wall a lug on the pin rides up a small ramp, which pulls the head tight, drops into a notch, and a stop keeps it from turning further. The head shows at a glance which pins are locked. To remove one, turn it back along the slot (it takes a firm twist) and pull it out. They're the same as the pins on the VoiceLabels case. If a pin is too stiff to turn, lower RAMP_H (0.4 mm) in enclosure.scad; if it doesn't click firmly, raise it a little, or print the pins in PETG.
