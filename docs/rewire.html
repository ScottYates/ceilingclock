Update an older breadboard

For a breadboard built before firmware 2.0 on one breadboard: everything up to the amplifier from the build guide. Since 2.0 the ceiling projector is a second TM1637 display (the Nokia LCD, sunrise ring and projector LED are gone from the design), and two alarm buttons switch the alarms on and off. Nothing on your board changes; you add those two things, and the DS3231 clock module, which now fits on one breadboard too. The alarm buttons' lights need LEDs, so on the breadboard they are left out; they come with the 16 mm lit buttons later. Allow 25 minutes. The pictures show your board turned with row 1 on the left.

1

Your board now, and clearing space

⏱ 5 min

Your board has everything up to the amplifier from the build guide, on one breadboard: the front display, buttons A and B, the light sensor, the 5 V supply and the amplifier. The old projector parts (Nokia LCD, sunrise ring, projector LED) were never wired, so nothing has to come out. ESP32 pins 8 to 13 are free, and the right-hand end of the board (rows 49 to 60) is empty: that's where the new parts go.

  1. Unplug the USB cable from the computer and the 5 V adapter.
  2. Lift the computer's USB cable off the breadboard and lay it to the right of the ESP32's USB port, away from rows 25 to 60. It lies across the holes the alarm buttons use.
  3. If a loose wire runs from beside the ESP32 to a foil tab (a probe point from testing the amplifier), take it out. It isn't part of the clock.
2

Projector display (second TM1637)

⏱ 5 min

The ceiling projector is a second four-digit display: in the clock, a lens images its glowing digits onto the ceiling. It wires up exactly like the front display, on two other pins. It needs four new female–male jumper wires.

⚠️ Don't mix up the two displays: the front one stays on pins 4 and 5.

Plug the USB cable back into the computer.

Connect

The two data wires go in the holes in front of the yellow strip, beside pins 11 and 12. That's rows 17 (pin 11) and 18 (pin 12); counting back from the 5V pin's hole, they're the 4th and 3rd. Wire colours don't matter; the pictures use the guide's.

✅ Test it: The display shows the time, flipped: the clock pre-flips the digits so they read correctly after the lens and mirror. Turn off Mirror left to right and Mirror top to bottom under Settings › Ceiling picture to read it on the bench, then set them again once it projects. That section shows both what the module lights up and what the ceiling should read.
Not working?
Display stays blank
Is the projector on? Its setting may be Only at night or Off: press Show the ceiling time for 15 seconds under Settings › Ceiling picture. Otherwise CLK and DIO are swapped, or VCC isn't on the + rail.
The front display changes instead
The two displays' wires are crossed: the front one uses GPIO 4/5, the projector GPIO 12/11.
3

Alarm buttons

⏱ 10 min

In the finished clock these are two 16 mm buttons with a light inside: one switches alarm 1 on and off, the other alarm 2. On the breadboard a small push button stands in for each, without the lights: they need LEDs, and the clock works the same without them. The lights come back with the 16 mm buttons on the perfboard. Unplug the USB cable while you add the buttons.

⚠️ Rows 49 and 55 are next to the light sensor's resistor (row 48). Count carefully: the purple wire goes in row 49, not 48.

Place the parts

  1. Push button 1 across the gap in the middle: two legs in row 49, two in row 51, one each side of the gap.
  2. Push button 2 across the gap in the middle: two legs in row 55, two in row 57, one each side of the gap.

Connect

✅ Test it: Plug the USB cable back in. Press alarm button 1: the front display shows 1 on and then alarm 1's time, and on the clock's page alarm 1's switch turns on. Press it again: the display shows 1 oF and the switch turns off. Do the same with button 2. Then press button A (snooze) with no alarm ringing: the display shows which alarms are on, for example AL12 (both), AL1- (only 1) or AL-- (none).
Not working?
Button does nothing
Check the wires go to diagonally opposite legs (rows 49 and 51, 55 and 57) and that the button straddles the gap.
4

Clock module (DS3231)

⏱ 5 min

The battery-backed clock that keeps time when Wi-Fi or power drops. In the finished clock it uses ESP32 pins 1 and 2, but on one breadboard those are on the covered side. So here it goes on pins 18 and 3, on the front edge: the firmware (2.3.1 or later) looks there when the module isn't on 1 and 2. Your clock already runs that firmware.

Connect

Pin 18 is row 11 and pin 3 is row 13: the two empty holes in front of the yellow strip, just right of the amplifier's three wires (rows 8 to 10).

✅ Test it: Press RST on the ESP32. The clock's page shows RTC backup ok, and the serial log says DS3231 on pins 18 and 3. Turn your router's Wi-Fi off and press RST again: the time is still right.
Not working?
Still says "no RTC"
SDA and SCL swapped (SDA goes beside pin 18, SCL beside pin 3), VCC on 5 V instead of 3.3 V, or firmware older than 2.3.1.
ZS-042 (larger) board
It charges its battery. With a non-rechargeable CR2032, remove the 200 Ω resistor or diode beside the holder first.
On the perfboard
Wire it to pins 1 and 2 as the wiring page shows; the firmware tries those first.
5

Check the whole board

⏱ 10 min

Everything on the board, as the build guide has it. Go through the list once with the power off and tick each wire as you find it. Your colours may differ (your amplifier wires are purple, blue and white, and the light sensor's wire is red); what matters is where each end goes.

✅ Test it: Plug in the USB cable and the 5 V adapter. The front display shows the time; the projector display shows it flipped; alarm buttons 1 and 2 switch their alarms (1 on, 1 oF); button B switches the front display (hold 3 s for the projector); button A shows which alarms are on (AL12); under Sounds, Play on beep.wav beeps four times.

Where to go from here

  1. See it on the ceiling. In a dark room, hold the lens about 106 mm above the projector display and focus it on the ceiling (bonus step in the build guide). Set the flips under Settings › Ceiling picture.
  2. Get the parts the breadboard doesn't use (parts list): the two 16 mm lit buttons for the alarms, the 16 mm buttons for A and B, the perfboard, the lens and the mirror.
  3. Print the case while you wait (assembly step 1). It already has the two alarm-button holes in the lid and the projector display's holder in the barrel.
  4. Build the electronics board (assembly step 4): the same connections, soldered on perfboard. The two alarm lights go in (pins 10 and 13, with the 16 mm lit buttons), and the DS3231 moves to pins 1 and 2 as the wiring page shows. From then on the 5 V supply powers the ESP32 too, and firmware updates go over Wi-Fi.
  5. Assemble the case: speaker, front display, the projector display and lens in the barrel, the lid buttons, then focus and aim (assembly steps 2–9). The wiring page has the full pin list.
