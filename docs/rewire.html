Rewire an older breadboard

For a breadboard built from the first version of the guide, on one breadboard: with the Nokia LCD, the sunrise ring and the projector LED. Since firmware 2.0 the projector is a second TM1637 display, the ring and LED are gone, and two alarm buttons with lights switch the alarms on and off. Allow 45 minutes. The pictures show your board turned with row 1 on the left, as in the build guide.

1

Unplug everything

⏱ 1 min

Unplug the USB cable from the computer and the 5 V adapter. Every change below is made with the power off.

This is your board now, built from the old guide: the Nokia LCD, the sunrise ring and the projector LED on the right. Your ESP32 already runs the new firmware (2.3.0), which no longer uses the LCD, the ring or the LED.

2

Take out the sunrise ring

⏱ 5 min

The sunrise light is gone from the design. Take out its four wires, the 330 Ω resistor and the big 1000 µF capacitor in the bottom rails. The resistor in row 50–54 sits exactly where the first alarm light goes in step 6.

  1. Pull out the 330 Ω resistor (orange-orange-brown) from c50 and c54.
  2. Pull out the 1000 µF capacitor (the big blue one) from the bottom rails. The small 100 µF one by the amplifier stays.
  3. Put the ring away; it isn't used any more.
Not working?
Two capacitors in the bottom rails: which is which?
The 1000 µF is the bigger one, at about row 23. The 100 µF that stays is at about row 19, next to the amplifier's wires.
3

Take out the projector LED and its switch

⏱ 5 min

The projector no longer needs a lamp: the new projector display lights its own digits. Take out the MOSFET module, the 3 W LED and the 4.7 Ω resistor with their seven wires.

  1. Pull out the 4.7 Ω 2 W resistor (the big blue one) from h56 and h60. It's next to where the second alarm button goes.
  2. Unscrew the wires from the MOSFET module's terminals. Keep the module and LED for something else; the clock doesn't use them.
Not working?
A wire is soldered to the LED star
Leave it on the star; just pull its other end out of the breadboard or terminal.
4

Unplug the LCD

⏱ 5 min

The new projector display needs only two data wires: GPIO 12 and 11, the LCD's old CLK and DIN wires. They stay, along with its + and − wires. The other four come out: RST, CE and DC (GPIO 8, 10 and 9; the alarm buttons use those pins now) and the backlight wire.

⚠️ Leave the yellow wire in the row of pin 12 and the green one in the row of pin 11. Those two go to the new display in the next step.

Then pull the female ends of the other four wires (yellow CLK, green DIN, orange VCC, black GND) off the LCD's pins, and leave their other ends in the breadboard. Put the LCD away.

5

Fit the second TM1637 as the projector

⏱ 5 min

The projector is now a second four-digit display, the same kind as the front one. A lens in the clock images its digits onto the ceiling. It plugs onto the four wires the LCD had, with the breadboard ends where they already are.

⚠️ Don't swap the two displays' wires: the front display stays on pins 4 and 5.

Plug the USB cable back into the computer (the 5 V adapter can wait).

✅ Test it: The display shows the time, flipped: the clock pre-flips the digits so they read correctly after the lens and mirror. Turn off Mirror left to right and Mirror top to bottom under Settings › Ceiling picture to read it on the bench, then set them again once it projects. That section shows both what the module lights up and what the ceiling should read.
Not working?
Display stays blank
Is the projector on? Its setting may be Only at night or Off: press Show the ceiling time for 15 seconds under Settings › Ceiling picture. Otherwise CLK and DIO are swapped, or VCC isn't on the + rail.
The front display changes instead
The two displays' wires are crossed: the front one uses GPIO 4/5, the projector GPIO 12/11.
6

Add the two alarm buttons and their lights

⏱ 15 min

In the finished clock these are two 16 mm buttons with a light inside: one switches alarm 1 on and off, the other alarm 2. On the breadboard a small push button and an ordinary LED stand in for each. The LED's long leg is +. The 220 Ω resistor limits its current; without it the LED and the ESP32 pin are overloaded. They go in the rows the ring's and LED's resistors used. Unplug the USB cable while you add them.

⚠️ Each wire to the ESP32 goes into the free hole beside the pin, in the column nearest you (the same column as every other ESP32 wire).

Place the parts

  1. Push button 1 across the gap in the middle: two legs in row 49, two in row 51, one each side of the gap.
  2. LED 1: the long leg (+) in c52, the short leg in c53. This one has a right way round.
  3. 220 Ω resistor (bands red-red-brown, or red-red-black-black on 5-band resistors): one leg in a53, the same row as the LED's short leg; the other in the bottom − rail at row 54. Either way round.
  4. Push button 2 across the gap in the middle: two legs in row 55, two in row 57, one each side of the gap.
  5. LED 2: the long leg (+) in c58, the short leg in c59. This one has a right way round.
  6. 220 Ω resistor (bands red-red-brown, or red-red-black-black on 5-band resistors): one leg in a59, the same row as the LED's short leg; the other in the bottom − rail at row 60. Either way round.

Connect

✅ Test it: Plug the USB cable back in. Press alarm button 1: the front display shows 1 on and then alarm 1's time, LED 1 lights for 3 seconds, and on the clock's page alarm 1's switch turns on. Press it again: the display shows 1 oF and LED 1 blinks twice. Do the same with button 2. Then press button A (snooze): the LEDs of the alarms that are on light up for 3 seconds.
Not working?
Button does nothing
Check the wires go to diagonally opposite legs (rows 49 and 51, 55 and 57) and that the button straddles the gap.
The page changes but the LED never lights
The LED is the wrong way round (long leg goes in the row with the wire from the ESP32), or the resistor isn't reaching the − rail.
The LED is very bright, or the ESP32 resets
The resistor is missing or isn't 220 Ω. Check the bands.
7

Check the whole board

⏱ 10 min

Everything on the board, as the current build guide has it. Go through the list once with the power off; tick each wire as you find it. Anything left over that isn't on the list (a stray wire in rows 23–29 of the rails, or in rows 49–60) should come out.

✅ Test it: Plug in the USB cable and the 5 V adapter. The front display shows the time; the projector display shows it flipped; alarm buttons 1 and 2 switch their alarms and their lights blink; button B switches the front display (hold 3 s for the projector); button A shows which alarms are on; under Sounds, Play on beep.wav beeps four times.

Where to go from here

  1. See it on the ceiling. In a dark room, hold the lens about 106 mm above the projector display and focus it on the ceiling (bonus step in the build guide). Set the flips under Settings › Ceiling picture.
  2. Get the parts the breadboard doesn't use (parts list): the two 16 mm lit buttons for the alarms, the 16 mm buttons for A and B, the DS3231 clock module, the perfboard, the lens and the mirror.
  3. Print the case while you wait (assembly step 1). It already has the two alarm-button holes in the lid and the projector display's holder in the barrel.
  4. Build the electronics board (assembly step 4): the same connections, soldered on perfboard, with the DS3231. On one breadboard there's no room for it, and it closes the one gap left in the alarms: after a power cut while the internet is also down, the clock only knows the time if the DS3231 is fitted. From then on the 5 V supply powers the ESP32 too, and firmware updates go over Wi-Fi.
  5. Assemble the case: speaker, front display, the projector display and lens in the barrel, the lid buttons, then focus and aim (assembly steps 2–9). The wiring page has the full pin list.
