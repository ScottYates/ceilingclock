Breadboard test

Before soldering the perfboard or printing the case, wire everything up on a breadboard and check each part works. Add one module at a time and test it before adding the next, so if something doesn't work you know exactly where to look. It takes an evening, and every wire goes to the same pin as in the finished clock.

Try the web page first. The live demo runs the same firmware in your browser, so you can learn the settings before any hardware is involved.

What you need

  • Everything on the parts list except the printed parts. The LCD can be tested before you cut its window.
  • Two full-size breadboards pushed side by side. The ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 is too wide for one: it would cover all but one hole on one side. Straddle it across the gap between the two boards so each row of its pins has free holes beside it. A single wide "ESP32" breadboard also works.
  • Jumper wires (male–male, plus a few male–female for modules with header pins).
  • A USB-C data cable from your computer to the board's port marked USB. That's where the firmware sends its log; flashing works on either port.
  • The 5 V 3 A supply and the USB-C breakout, for the LED, ring and amplifier.
  • Something metal to sit the LED star on while it's lit (an old heatsink, a steel ruler, a can lid).

Power on the bench

While testing, the ESP32 runs from your computer's USB, so you can flash it and watch its log. The external 5 V supply feeds only the parts that need real current: the amplifier, the sunrise ring and the projector LED. The ESP32's own 3V3 pin feeds the logic modules on a separate rail.

Generated from firmware/src/pins.h by hardware/make_breadboard_svg.py. The full signal diagram is on the wiring page.

Two rules: join the two GND rails (otherwise the signals have no common reference and the ring and amplifier misbehave), and don't connect the external 5 V to the ESP32's 5V pin while the computer is plugged in. Two supplies would fight through the board. In the finished clock the 5 V supply does feed that pin, and the computer stays unplugged (updates go over Wi-Fi).

Build it up, one module at a time

Unplug the USB cable each time you add wires, then plug it back in to test. GPIO numbers are the labels printed on the board.

Test the projected image

Once the LCD has its window cut (assembly step 5), you can check the optics on the bench before printing:

  1. In a dark room, point the LED up. About 10 mm above it, hold a piece of baking paper; about 30 mm above the LED, hold the LCD, glass up.
  2. Hold the lens about 106 mm above the LCD and look at the ceiling. Move the lens up and down a little until the digits are sharp. That distance is what the threaded focus ring adjusts in the finished clock.
  3. Set Mirror left/right and Flip projection so it reads right from where you lie. In the clock the 45° mirror adds one more flip, so expect to change Mirror left/right once it's built.

If something doesn't work

ProblemCheck
Nothing in the serial monitorUse the port marked USB, at 115200 baud. Some USB-C cables only carry power.
The ESP32 resets when the ring or LED turns onThe GND rails aren't joined, or the 5 V supply is weak. Check the 1000 µF capacitor at the ring.
Front display blankVCC must go to 3V3; CLK and DIO swapped?
LCD blank or all blackLCD contrast on the settings page; check RST and CE. Breadboard contacts on cheap header pins can be loose: wiggle them.
Ring flickers or shows wrong coloursShort data wire, the 330 Ω in series, the capacitor at the ring. If it persists, add a 74AHCT125 level shifter on the data line.
No soundThe amplifier needs the 5 V supply plugged in. Check BCLK/LRC/DIN aren't swapped, and the speaker leads.
"no RTC" on the pageSDA and SCL swapped, or the module on 5 V instead of 3V3.

When everything passes, move the same connections onto the perfboard (assembly step 4). In the clock, the 5 V rail also feeds the ESP32's 5V pin, so from then on leave the computer unplugged and update over Wi-Fi.
