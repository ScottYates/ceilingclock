Firmware & everyday use

Flash once over USB. After that, updates, alarms, sounds and display settings all happen from your phone.

See it running

The real firmware running in the PC simulator (no hardware), showing the phone page's live view through an alarm: ringing, snooze, then flipping the projection upside down and back. The ceiling picture stays readable while the projector module's own digits are mirrored.

Flash the firmware

Easiest, no install (Chrome or Edge on a computer):

  1. Download ceilingclock-esp32s3-factory.bin (2.5 MB). It's the complete image: bootloader, partition table and firmware.
  2. Connect the ESP32-S3 by its port marked USB, with the 5 V supply unplugged.
  3. Open Espressif's web flasher at espressif.github.io/esptool-js, set the baud rate to 921600 and click Connect. Pick the board's serial port. If it doesn't connect, hold BOOT, tap RST, release BOOT and try again.
  4. Under Program, set the flash address to 0x0, choose the downloaded file and click Program. When it finishes, press RST.

Or build it yourself with PlatformIO:

  1. Install VS Code and the PlatformIO IDE extension.
  2. Download the repository (zip) and open the firmware/ folder in VS Code.
  3. Connect the board as above and click the PlatformIO Upload arrow. The first build downloads the toolchain and libraries, which takes a few minutes.
  4. Open the serial monitor (115200 baud) to watch it start.

Connect it to Wi-Fi

  1. On first boot the projector shows WIFI SETUP.
  2. On your phone, join the Wi-Fi network CeilingClock-Setup. A setup page opens (or go to 192.168.4.1). Pick your home network and enter its password.
  3. Once it's connected, open http://ceilingclock.local/. On Android, use the IP address shown in the serial monitor or your router, and reserve it in the router so it doesn't change.

Later updates: upload ceilingclock-esp32s3-update.bin (or your own .pio/build/esp32s3/firmware.bin) in the page's Firmware update box. Alarms and sounds are kept.

Try the web page

Live demo: the actual firmware compiled to WebAssembly, running in your browser with simulated hardware. The page is the same one the clock serves. A control bar lets you fast-forward time, press the buttons and change the room light. It starts in your time zone and forgets everything when you reload.

On your computer: the simulator also builds as a normal Linux or Mac program, keeping its files in a folder. Run pio pkg install in firmware/, then make run in firmware/sim/, and open localhost:8080.

The clock's web page

Open http://ceilingclock.local/ (or the clock's IP address) on your phone. Every change saves itself; there are no Save buttons.

Password and trusted computers

Under Settings › Security and updates you can set a password: then the clock's page, its settings and firmware updates ask for user name admin and that password. Computers on the Trusted computers list don't need it; press Trust this computer on the one you use most. The clock knows them by their address on your home network, so give each a fixed address in your router (often called a DHCP reservation). The clock uses plain http, so treat the password as a lock against accidents and visitors, not against someone snooping on your Wi-Fi.

Battery

With the battery base fitted (firmware 2.6.0 or later), the clock keeps running when the power goes, about three days on one 13400 mAh pack. A Battery line on the clock's page shows the charge, whether it's charging, and on battery about how many hours are left.

  • Charge limit. Under Settings › Battery, from 50% to 100% in steps of 5; the default is 80%. Lithium cells kept full wear out faster, and a clock spends nearly all its life on the mains, so 80% gives a longer-lived pack for a little less runtime. The clock stops charging at the limit and starts again when the pack drops 3% below it. At 100% the charger works on its own.
  • On battery both displays use their night brightness, to make the pack last. Alarms ring as normal.
  • Messages on the front display: bAtt when the power goes, PLug when it comes back, and bA 9 (the charge in %) every 10 minutes once the pack is below 10%.
  • No gauge: if the fuel gauge stops answering, the clock lets the charger charge to full and assumes it's on mains power, so nothing ever stops it charging by mistake.

Making sure alarms go off

Firmware 2.3.0 was checked for every way an alarm could fail to ring. What it does about each:

If…The clock…
The clock jumps forward over an alarm (the first time sync after starting from the backup clock), or is busy for a whileRings it anyway: each check covers everything since the last one, up to 30 minutes back.
The power comes back after an alarm time (with the DS3231 fitted)Rings alarms that were due in the last 15 minutes.
It crashes, restarts, or is updated while an alarm rings or is snoozedCarries on ringing, or keeps the snooze, after the restart. It also keeps the time and which alarms have rung, in memory that a reset doesn't clear.
The program locks upA watchdog restarts it within 30 seconds, and it carries on as above.
The alarm's sound file is damaged, or plays but nothing comes outSwitches to the built-in beep within 10 seconds.
An internet radio or podcast won't start, stalls, or dropsPlays the backup sound, then the beep if that fails too.
Even the beep won't play (the sound system is stuck)Restarts itself and rings with the beep (up to 3 times).
The router starts after the clock (common after a power cut)Opens the setup hotspot for 3 minutes, then keeps rejoining your Wi-Fi every 30 seconds.
Settings are being saved when the power goesKeeps a backup copy and checks each save, so the alarms are never lost.
Settings are saved while an alarm ringsKeeps ringing, even if that alarm is edited or deleted. Use Stop.
An alarm is set inside the hour daylight saving skips or repeatsRings once either way. At 2:30 on the night the clocks go forward (2:30 never happens), it rings at 3:30. At 1:30 on the night they go back (1:30 happens twice), it rings at the second 1:30.
An alarm's volume is set very lowNever rings quieter than about 30% (6 of 21), and every step is audible. A fade-in always finishes within the first third of the ringing time.

The one case it can't cover: a power cut while the internet is also down, with no DS3231 fitted. When the power returns the clock doesn't know the time, so it can't ring until the internet is back. Fit the DS3231 (in the parts list) so the clock always knows the time.

All of this is tested. firmware/test/test_alarm_schedule.cpp runs every weekday pattern and alarm time through three years in seven time zones, with irregular checks and clock jumps, including the hour that daylight saving skips or repeats. firmware/test/sim_alarm_e2e.py runs 19 failure scenarios against the simulator, including a frozen main loop and both daylight-saving nights. The simulator compiles every firmware source file exactly as it is built for the clock, with the ESP32's own date code (newlib) in place of the PC's, so what passes there is what runs on the clock.

Troubleshooting

SymptomFix
Build error undefined reference to vtable for DecoderMake sure src/audio_lib_fix.cpp is present. The audio library (pinned to 4.0.1 in platformio.ini) leaves two functions undefined, and that file supplies them.
Projector and front display show -- --No Wi-Fi and no RTC time yet. Check the DS3231 wiring; the page should say "RTC backup ok".
Ceiling digits too faintThe projected digits are meant for a dark room. Set the projector's brightness to 7, focus carefully, and keep the lens clean. Light from a lamp or a window will wash them out.
Ceiling digits lean the wrong wayMany TM1637 modules have slanted digits; mirroring flips the slant. Nothing is wrong.
No soundCheck the amplifier has 5 V and the speaker is connected, then press Play on beep.wav. If your own file won't play, re-save it as 44.1 kHz MP3.
Resets when the alarm startsThe power supply is too weak or the wires too thin. Try a lower alarm volume, and check the 100 µF capacitor is at the amplifier.

Build status: the firmware compiles for the ESP32-S3 (74% of program space, 20% of RAM) and runs in the PC simulator. It runs on a breadboard build (version 2.0.0, with both displays and the amplifier).
